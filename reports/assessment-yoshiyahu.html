The Lamp and the Name

יאשיהו דוד עזריאל לישאביץ · 1107

Your name adds up to 1107. The Torahscope search returned 264 places in the Tanakh, the Mishnah and the Mishneh Torah where words, phrases or whole verses share that number. This assessment reads those results together, the way a dream is read: as images that keep returning, set against the Kabbalistic Tree of Life and the psychology of Carl Jung.

Method

Three lenses on one list

Jung called his way of reading a dream amplification: take each image, gather everything the tradition says about it, and watch which themes keep returning. The search results are treated the same way here. A phrase that turns up once is noise. A theme that turns up in the Torah, the Prophets, the Mishnah and Rambam is a signal about what your name gathers.

Torah

What each passage actually says, in its own context. Every interpretation starts there.

Kabbalah

Where each theme sits on the Tree of Life, the map of ten sefirot through which, in Kabbalistic thought, the divine flows into the world and into a person.

Jung

What each theme says about the psyche: the Self at the centre, the persona we show, the shadow we don't, and the long work Jung called individuation.

Seven threads run through the results. Each chapter below follows one.

The map

Your results on the Tree of Life

One result was placed on each of the ten sefirot, chosen because it speaks most directly to that sefirah's meaning. The centre of the Tree, Tiferet, is the only sefirah whose own name appears in your results, word for word.

KeterThe crown, will beyond reach. אֵין דֵּי "not sufficient": nothing offered is enough (Isaiah 40:16).
ChokhmahThe first flash of insight. וּמְסַתְּרָתָא "and hidden things," which God reveals (Daniel 2:22).
BinahUnderstanding that gives form. תּוֹרֹתָיו הוֹדַע "make known its laws," the plan of the house (Ezekiel 43:11).
ChesedGiving. הָרַבִּים תִּתֵּן "Your great mercies You gave" (Nehemiah 9:27).
GevurahRestraint. עָרְלָתוֹ אֶת fruit you must wait three years to eat (Leviticus 19:23).
TiferetBeauty and balance at the centre. וְתִפְאַרְתֶּךָ "and Your glory" (Isaiah 63:15).
NetzachEndurance. הַשְּׁאֵרִית הַנִּמְצָאָה "the remnant that is left" (2 Kings 19:4).
HodPraise and acknowledgment. הַלְלוּ אֶת־שֵׁם "praise the name" (Psalm 113:1).
YesodFoundation and channel. The stones for the breastplate, engraved with names (Exodus 25:7).
MalchutKingship, speech, presence. אֲשֶׁר תְּדַבֵּר "what you shall speak" (Exodus 4:12).

Two traditional details fit this map. The Tree has 22 paths, one for each Hebrew letter, and your full name is exactly 22 letters long. And 1107 reduces to 1 + 1 + 0 + 7 = 9, the number of Yesod, the foundation, which is where the verse about engraved names sits.

Thread one · The Self
תִּפְאֶרֶת

A centre you long for

הַבֵּט מִשָּׁמַיִם וּרְאֵה מִזְּבֻל קָדְשְׁךָ וְתִפְאַרְתֶּךָ אַיֵּה קִנְאָתְךָ וּגְבוּרֹתֶךָ הֲמוֹן מֵעֶיךָ וְרַחֲמֶיךָ אֵלַי הִתְאַפָּקוּ
Look down from heaven, and see, even from Thy holy and glorious habitation; Where is Thy zeal and Thy mighty acts, The yearning of Thy heart and Thy compassions, Now restrained toward me?
Isaiah 63:15 · word match
יַעֲשֶׂה וְיִשְׁנֶה וִישַׁלֵּשׁ בְּמַעֲשִׂים שֶׁעוֹשֶׂה עַל פִּי הַדֵּעוֹת הָאֶמְצָעִיּוֹת … נִקְרֵאת דֶּרֶךְ זוֹ דֶּרֶךְ ה׳
He should do, repeat, and do a third time the acts that follow the middle dispositions, until they come easily… This path is called the way of God.
Mishneh Torah, Human Dispositions 1:7 · phrase match

Torah. Isaiah 63:15 is a cry, not a statement. The prophet asks God to look down from His "holy and glorious habitation," and asks where His yearning has gone: "now restrained toward me?" The glory is real and it feels far away.

Kabbalah. Tiferet is the heart of the Tree. It holds Chesed (giving without limit) and Gevurah (limit and judgment) in balance, and it is associated with Jacob and with the Torah itself. Rambam's "middle dispositions," also in your results, are the ethical form of the same idea: neither extreme, and the middle path is called the way of God.

Jung. The Self, in Jung's sense, is the centre of the whole personality, larger than the ego and holding its opposites together. People usually meet it first as a longing, a sense that there is a centre and you are not quite in it. Your results put that longing at the centre of the map. Rambam's advice for reaching it is practical: repeat the balanced act until it stops costing effort.

Where in your life do you feel the "yearning restrained," a centre you can sense but not yet stand in?

Thread two · Persona and Self
יְסוֹד

The names you carry

וַיְהִי בָהֶם מִבְּנֵי יְהוּדָה דָּנִיֵּאל חֲנַנְיָה מִישָׁאֵל וַעֲזַרְיָה
Now among these were, of the children of Judah, Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
Daniel 1:6 · the whole verse equals 1107
וַיָּשֶׂם לָהֶם שַׂר הַסָּרִיסִים שֵׁמוֹת וַיָּשֶׂם לְדָנִיֵּאל בֵּלְטְשַׁאצַּר וְלַחֲנַנְיָה שַׁדְרַךְ וּלְמִישָׁאֵל מֵישַׁךְ וְלַעֲזַרְיָה עֲבֵד נְגוֹ
And the chief of the officers gave names unto them: unto Daniel he gave the name of Belteshazzar; and to Hananiah, of Shadrach; and to Mishael, of Meshach; and to Azariah, of Abed-nego.
Daniel 1:7 · the next verse

Torah. Four of your six whole-verse matches are about names. Daniel 1:6 lists four young men by their Hebrew names, one of them Azariah, a close cousin of your own Azriel. The very next verse takes those names away and gives them Babylonian ones. Exodus 25:7 lists the stones of the High Priest's breastplate, which Exodus 28:9 says were engraved with "the names of the children of Israel." Psalms 113:1 and 135:1 both call out "praise the name." Among the phrases, אַנְשֵׁי שֵׁמוֹת, "men of names," appears in 1 Chronicles 5:24, in the same verse as a man named Azriel. And Ruth 4:14 has the women of Bethlehem blessing a newborn: "let his name be famous in Israel." Three verses later, that child is named as the grandfather of David.

Kabbalah. In Jewish thought a person's Hebrew name is understood as a channel for their soul. Yesod, the foundation, is the sefirah of channels and connection, and it is where the engraved stones sit on your map.

Jung. Jung's persona is the face we show the world, the role and the name it knows us by. It is necessary, not false. The danger is mistaking it for the whole person. Daniel served the Babylonian court for decades under the name Belteshazzar and stayed Daniel. Your name has the same shape: three Hebrew names, a king, a king and "God is my help," joined to a family name carried through the diaspora. The work your results point to is holding both, the name the world uses and the name that is engraved.

Which of your four names do you live from most of the time, and which one is waiting to be lived?

Thread three · The gift
מַלְכוּת

A mouth that is taught

וְעַתָּה לֵךְ וְאָנֹכִי אֶהְיֶה עִם־פִּיךָ וְהוֹרֵיתִיךָ אֲשֶׁר תְּדַבֵּר
Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt speak.’
Exodus 4:12 · phrase match
כּוֹתֵב לְעַצְמוֹ זִכְרוֹן בַּשְּׁמוּעוֹת שֶׁשָּׁמַע מֵרַבּוֹתָיו, וְהוּא מְלַמֵּד עַל פֶּה בָּרַבִּים
[In each generation, the head of the court] writes for himself a record of the traditions he heard from his teachers, and teaches it aloud to the many.
Mishneh Torah, Introduction (Transmission of the Oral Law) · phrase match

Torah. Speech is the most common thread in your Tanakh results: "that you have spoken" (first in Genesis 19:21, and 18 times across the Bible), "what you shall speak" (Exodus 4:12), "make known its laws… and write it in their sight" (Ezekiel 43:11). Exodus 4:12 comes right after Moses objects that he is "heavy of mouth." God's answer is not to fix the mouth. It is "I will be with your mouth."

Kabbalah. The prayer Patach Eliyahu, from the Tikkunei Zohar, says "Malchut is the mouth, and we call it the Oral Torah." Malchut, kingship, is also the sefirah traditionally linked to King David, your second name. Rambam's line about each generation writing down what it heard and teaching it aloud is the Oral Torah in action, and it is close to what Torahscope itself does.

Jung. Jung's figure of the wounded healer fits here: the gift arrives through the place that feels weakest. Moses' reluctance is part of his authority. If speaking, teaching or explaining feels like both your calling and your difficulty, your results suggest that is the normal shape of this gift, not a sign it isn't yours.

What do you know that you have not yet said aloud to the people who need it?

Thread four · The shadow
גְּבוּרָה

The fire in the lips, the thorn in the eye

אִישׁ בְּלִיַּעַל כֹּרֶה רָעָה וְעַל־שְׂפָתוֹ כְּאֵשׁ צָרָבֶת
An ungodly man diggeth up evil, And in his lips there is as a burning fire.
Proverbs 16:27 · phrase match
וְאִם־לֹא תוֹרִישׁוּ אֶת־יֹשְׁבֵי הָאָרֶץ מִפְּנֵיכֶם וְהָיָה אֲשֶׁר תּוֹתִירוּ מֵהֶם לְשִׂכִּים בְּעֵינֵיכֶם וְלִצְנִינִם בְּצִדֵּיכֶם וְצָרֲרוּ אֶתְכֶם עַל־הָאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם יֹשְׁבִים בָּהּ
But if ye will not drive out the inhabitants of the land from before you, then shall those that ye let remain of them be as thorns in your eyes, and as pricks in your sides, and they shall harass you in the land wherein ye dwell.
Numbers 33:55 · phrase match
לָשׁוּב מִן הַכַּעַס וּמִן הָאֵיבָה וּמִן הַקִּנְאָה וּמִן הַהִתּוּל … וְאֵלּוּ הָעֲוֹנוֹת קָשִׁים מֵאוֹתָן שֶׁיֵּשׁ בָּהֶן מַעֲשֶׂה
To turn back from anger, from enmity, from jealousy, from mockery… These sins are harder than those that involve a deed, because once a person is sunk in them it is hard to separate from them.
Mishneh Torah, Repentance 7:3 · phrase match

Torah. Every gift in your results has a shadow beside it. Next to the taught mouth stands the mouth that burns: "in his lips there is as a burning fire." Next to the prophets stand those who "mocked the messengers of God… and scoffed at His prophets" (2 Chronicles 36:16). In Judges 15:2, a father gives his daughter away because "I verily thought that thou hadst utterly hated her": a whole tragedy built on a misread heart. And Psalm 141:4 is the prayer against it: "Incline not my heart to any evil thing."

Kabbalah. Gevurah, strength and judgment, is necessary. Without it nothing has form. But in Kabbalah, judgment cut off from Chesed is where harshness begins. Speech is where Gevurah most easily turns harsh.

Jung. The shadow is what we don't see in ourselves, and Jung wrote that what is not made conscious tends to arrive from outside, as fate. Numbers 33:55 says the same in the language of the land: whatever you let remain unaddressed will be "thorns in your eyes, and pricks in your sides." Rambam's line is the most precise of all: the inner traits (anger, jealousy, mockery) are harder to leave than wrong actions, because you are sunk in them. The Judges verse names the shadow's favourite tool, projection: deciding what another person feels and acting on your guess.

When your words burn, what are you protecting? And what have you "let remain" that keeps returning as a thorn?

Thread five · Transformation
תִּקּוּן

Light from crushed olives

וְאַתָּה תְּצַוֶּה אֶת־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וְיִקְחוּ אֵלֶיךָ שֶׁמֶן זַיִת זָךְ כָּתִית לַמָּאוֹר לְהַעֲלֹת נֵר תָּמִיד
And thou shalt command the children of Israel, that they bring unto thee pure olive oil beaten for the light, to cause a lamp to burn continually.
Exodus 27:20 · the same phrase also matches in Mishnah Menachot 8:5 and Mishneh Torah, Things Forbidden on the Altar 7:10
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה פְּסָל־לְךָ שְׁנֵי־לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים כָּרִאשֹׁנִים וְכָתַבְתִּי עַל־הַלֻּחֹת אֶת־הַדְּבָרִים אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ עַל־הַלֻּחֹת הָרִאשֹׁנִים אֲשֶׁר שִׁבַּרְתָּ
And the LORD said unto Moses: ‘Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first; and I will write upon the tables the words that were on the first tables, which thou didst break.
Exodus 34:1 · phrase match, 7 occurrences

Torah. כָּתִית לַמָּאוֹר, "beaten for the light," is the one phrase in your results found in all three texts: the Torah's command, the Mishnah's ruling on which oil is purest, and Rambam's law. The oil for the Temple's eternal lamp had to come from olives that were crushed, not pressed for volume. Beside it stands Exodus 34:1: "hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first… which thou didst break." The second tablets are carved by Moses' own hand, after the first were shattered.

Kabbalah. Lurianic Kabbalah teaches that creation began with a breaking, shevirat ha-kelim, the shattering of vessels too small for the light. Human work is tikkun, repair. Your results even contain the phrase וְתַקָּנַת הָעוֹלָם, "the repair of the world" (Mishneh Torah, Murderer 2:4). Light that passes through breaking is the oldest image in the system.

Jung. Jung spent his last decades on alchemy because it described transformation in physical images: matter that has to be broken down before it can become gold. Crushed olives and recarved tablets are the same pattern. Your results suggest the clearest light in your life comes from what was pressed, not from what came easily, and that a broken first version is how the lasting one gets made.

What in your life was broken and then rebuilt by your own hand? What light came out of it?

Thread six · The unconscious
חָכְמָה

The one who reveals hidden things

הוּא גָּלֵא עַמִּיקָתָא וּמְסַתְּרָתָא יָדַע מָה בַחֲשׁוֹכָא וּנְהוֹרָא עִמֵּהּ שְׁרֵא
He revealeth the deep and secret things; He knoweth what is in the darkness, And the light dwelleth with Him.
Daniel 2:22 · word match
וְצַו אֶת־יְהוֹשֻׁעַ וְחַזְּקֵהוּ וְאַמְּצֵהוּ כִּי־הוּא יַעֲבֹר לִפְנֵי הָעָם הַזֶּה וְהוּא יַנְחִיל אוֹתָם אֶת־הָאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר תִּרְאֶה
But charge Joshua, and encourage him, and strengthen him; for he shall go over before this people, and he shall cause them to inherit the land which thou shalt see.’
Deuteronomy 3:28 · phrase match

Torah. Only three single words in the Tanakh equal 1107, and one of them is וּמְסַתְּרָתָא, "and hidden things." It comes from Daniel's prayer of thanks after the king's dream is revealed to him in a night vision. Seeing runs all through your results: "now shalt thou see" (Exodus 6:1), "the land which thou shalt see" (Deuteronomy 3:28), and its opposite, "I am not able to look up" (Psalm 40:13). The Deuteronomy verse is about Moses seeing a land that Joshua, not he, will lead the people into.

Kabbalah. Chokhmah, wisdom, is the first flash before anything has form. It is described as hidden, the point that becomes visible only when Binah gives it shape.

Jung. Daniel is the Bible's interpreter of dreams, which makes him the closest biblical figure to Jung's own work. His prayer is close to Jung's whole psychology in one sentence: there is a depth that is hidden, it can be revealed, and light lives there. The Deuteronomy verse adds a mature note. Some visions are meant to be seen by you and carried by someone after you. Jung described the second half of life as having its own task: after building yourself comes finding what your life is for beyond you.

What keeps returning in your dreams, or in the quiet moments before sleep? What have you seen that someone else may be the one to carry forward?

Thread seven · The anima
עֶזְרָה

Help, and the question at the well

כִּי־הָיִיתָ עֶזְרָתָה לִּי וּבְצֵל כְּנָפֶיךָ אֲרַנֵּן
For Thou hast been my help, And in the shadow of Thy wings do I rejoice.
Psalm 63:8 · phrase match
וַיֹּאמַר הָגָר שִׁפְחַת שָׂרַי אֵי־מִזֶּה בָאת וְאָנָה תֵלֵכִי וַתֹּאמֶר מִפְּנֵי שָׂרַי גְּבִרְתִּי אָנֹכִי בֹּרַחַת
And he said: ‘Hagar, Sarai’s handmaid, whence camest thou? and whither goest thou?’ And she said: ‘I flee from the face of my mistress Sarai.’
Genesis 16:8 · phrase match

Torah. Your third name, Azriel, means "my help is God," and help keeps surfacing in your results. Psalm 63:8 reads "Thou hast been my help." The Mishnah's match לְעֶזְרַת נָשִׁים names the Women's Court of the Temple, Ezrat Nashim, whose name shares its letters with עֶזְרָה, help. There on Sukkot they lit golden lamps so bright that, the Mishnah says, every courtyard in Jerusalem shone (Sukkah 5:2–3). Women's voices carry several of the most important moments: the women who bless Naomi's grandson in Ruth 4:14, Bathsheba's request in 1 Kings 2:21, and the angel's question to Hagar by the spring in the wilderness.

Kabbalah. In Genesis, the first woman is called עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ, "a help opposite him." Kabbalah identifies Malchut with the Shekhinah, the feminine presence of God, the receiving side of the Tree.

Jung. Jung's anima is the feminine inner figure in a man's psyche: the source of feeling, relationship and inspiration, and the guide toward the unconscious. In your results the feminine arrives as help and as a question. The angel asks Hagar, "Whence camest thou? and whither goest thou?" Jung could not have written a better summary of individuation: know where you came from, choose where you are going.

Whose help have you refused because it came from an unexpected direction? Where did you come from, and where are you going?

Coda · Leadership
נֶצַח

Not to bear it alone

וְיָרַדְתִּי וְדִבַּרְתִּי עִמְּךָ שָׁם וְאָצַלְתִּי מִן־הָרוּחַ אֲשֶׁר עָלֶיךָ וְשַׂמְתִּי עֲלֵיהֶם וְנָשְׂאוּ אִתְּךָ בְּמַשָּׂא הָעָם וְלֹא־תִשָּׂא אַתָּה לְבַדֶּךָ
And I will come down and speak with thee there; and I will take of the spirit which is upon thee, and will put it upon them; and they shall bear the burden of the people with thee, that thou bear it not thyself alone.
Numbers 11:17 · phrase match

Your results end, fittingly for a name made of two kings, with leadership. Moses is exhausted, and God takes some of the spirit that is on him and places it on seventy elders "that thou bear it not thyself alone." Nearby are the remnant that endures (2 Kings 19:4), the saviours given in time of trouble (Nehemiah 9:27), and Zechariah 4:9: "the hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it." Jung warned about inflation, the ego swelling to carry what belongs to something larger. The cure in your results is plain: share the spirit, finish what you founded, and wait for the fruit the way the Torah asks you to wait three years for a young tree (Leviticus 19:23).
