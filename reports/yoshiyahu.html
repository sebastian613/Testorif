יאשיהו Yoshiyahu · Josiah

52 appearances in 47 verses

Josiah was the king of Judah who rediscovered the Torah. In the eighteenth year of his reign, workers repairing the Temple found a scroll of the Law. Josiah read it aloud to the whole nation and renewed the covenant (2 Kings 22–23). He is also one of the few people in the Bible named in a prophecy generations before his birth.

דוד David

1,091 appearances in 925 verses

The shepherd, psalmist and king, and the root of the royal line that Josiah came from. The name is built on the root ד־ו־ד, "beloved." It appears in 22 books of the Tanakh, from Ruth to Chronicles, but never in the Torah itself as a name.

עזריאל Azriel

3 appearances, each a different man

"My help is God." A rare name: one clan leader of Manasseh, the father of a royal officer in Jeremiah's day, and the father of the tribal officer over Naphtali under David.

Words that share your numbers

Every distinct word in the Tanakh with the same gematria as each of your names, counted in full. These are the most meaningful of them. The number beside each word is how often it occurs.

עזריאל = 318 = אליעזר

Azriel and Eliezer have the same value, and they have the same meaning. Azriel is "my help is God," and Eliezer is "my God is help." The number 318 is also famous on its own: Abraham went to war with 318 men of his household (Genesis 14:14). The Talmud (Nedarim 32a) reads that number as Eliezer, Abraham's servant, alone.

לישאביץ = 443 = הַלֻּחֹת

Your family name has the same value as "the tablets," the stone tablets of the covenant. That word occurs 12 times in the Tanakh, and 11 of them are in the Torah. The same value belongs to בֶּאֱמֶת, "in truth."

צֹפֶן?

Is your name a hidden code?

"Torah codes" are words spelled out at equal spacing, like Azriel at every 294th letter above. Any long text contains thousands of these by pure chance, so a count only means something next to a fair comparison. We searched all spacings from every 2nd to every 1000th letter, forward and backward, then ran the same search on ten scrambled copies of the Torah.

NameFound in the TorahPlain scrambleFair scrambleVerdict

A plain scramble shuffles all 304,850 letters at once. With it, David looks like a signal, about five standard deviations above chance.

A fair scramble shuffles letters only within stretches of 5,000, keeping each passage's own mix of letters. Some passages are dense in ד and ו and others are not, and the plain scramble erases that. Measured fairly, David's excess drops by half, and Yoshiyahu falls a similar distance below chance. Two names drifting by the same amount in opposite directions is what noise looks like.

Our finding

Your names appear at equal spacings about as often as chance predicts. We report it plainly because the rest of this profile, the verses, the numbers and the letters, is real and you can check every line of it.

Method and sources

Torah letters: the Westminster Leningrad Codex as written (ketiv), via the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible project, 304,850 letters. Tanakh verses and word counts: the same codex as read aloud (qere), with the JPS 1917 English translation. Gematria counts every letter at its standard value, with final forms equal to their base letters. All counts are complete; nothing is sampled or capped. The hidden-sequence test uses ten scrambled copies for each control. The featured name verses and the commentary are editorial; every number is computed from the texts.
